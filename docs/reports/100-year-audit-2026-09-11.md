# 100-year audit — stopped during season 69

The requested 100-season run **failed to reach 100 seasons**. It completed 68 annual rollovers, from 2026/27 through 2093/94, then became stuck on **10 October 2094**. The managed player was marked retired halfway through an entered seniors event. Playing was disallowed, withdrawing was disallowed because two rounds had been completed, and advancing required playing or withdrawing.

The completed evidence supports a functioning CPU tournament world across several generations. It does not support an uninterrupted 100-year human career. No game state was altered to bypass the failure, and no replacement world was started.

The earlier five fixes are already implemented and separately tested in the working game. This baseline process had loaded the old source before those changes. See [post-audit corrections and verification](/C:/dev/snooker_career/docs/reports/long-career-fixes-2026-09-11.md).

## New failures and follow-up work

### 1. Retirement can deadlock an active tournament — confirmed, highest priority

The preserved terminal save contains **Seniors Tour - Event 3**, running 10–12 October 2094. Elliot, age 83, had won:

- Last 64: 4–3 against Eli Fischer.
- Last 32: 4–3 against Victor Garcia.

At Last 16 the state says `lateCareer.retired = true`. Replaying the public game actions on an isolated copy produces:

| Action | Actual response |
| --- | --- |
| Entry/playing eligibility | This player is retired from competitive events. |
| Withdraw | This event has already started and can no longer be withdrawn from. |
| Advance | Your tournament is ready. Complete preparation and play, or explicitly withdraw, before advancing. |

Neither action changes the date or cash. This reproduces the failure independently of the manager loop. [Exact reproduction](/C:/dev/snooker_career/artifacts/century-20260911/terminal-reproduction.json).

The baseline recalculates human retirement from age, card ownership, current ranking and seniors activity. It does not preserve retirement as a stable career decision. Changing rankings/activity can therefore change eligibility after entry. A fix should make retirement consistent, resolve an already-entered event safely, and provide a legal mid-event withdrawal/forfeit route when eligibility changes. Then rerun from preserved checkpoints before attempting another complete century test.

### 2. Retired-player handling is inconsistent in side events — confirmed gap, qualified observation

Ten accepted Amateur Club Pairs events occurred in five seasons whose opening state already marked the player retired: 2089/90 through 2093/94. CPU partner selection excludes retired players, but the human invitation, acceptance and match-start paths lack the equivalent retirement guard.

The annual check observes retirement at season opening, not at the exact acceptance transaction. Because retirement itself can change during a season, it cannot prove the flag was still true at every acceptance. The missing human checks and inconsistent lifecycle still need correcting together. [Incident records](/C:/dev/snooker_career/artifacts/century-20260911/report/life-findings.csv).

### 3. Human longevity and dominance need broader balance testing

This one managed career won **17 World Championships**, finished world number one **22 times**, and won its last World Championship at **50**. It earned a first professional card at 23 through the WSF/federation route and lost its card at 61. Overall rating rose from 63 at age 15 to 94 at 35–40, then fell to 91 at 50, 82 at 60, 71 at 70 and 63 at 80.

The CPU World Champions averaged **31.1 years old**; including the human, the average was **33.1**, with a 21–50 range. The very old champion results in this run were concentrated in the human career. This suggests reviewing human versus CPU development/decline and late-career eligibility. A single unusually successful seeded career is insufficient evidence for a universal nerf.

### 4. Large saves still warrant responsiveness work

The final save is **141,928,977 JSON bytes**. Baseline encoding took **83.2 seconds** in Node on this host, decoding 4.6 seconds, first repair 680 ms, second repair 649 ms and ranking rebuilding 24 ms. These are script timings, not an ordinary-laptop browser benchmark.

The storage-capacity fix does not by itself eliminate compression/loading costs. A complete 68-year UI responsiveness benchmark remains outstanding. The separate post-fix 25-year Save Manager test passed; it is not evidence of 100-year UI performance.

## What worked

### Tournament world, identities and statistics

Every scheduled event in each of the 68 complete annual ledgers was recorded, including the Championship League across the calendar boundary. All 572,628 scored bracket matches passed the configured format/score checks. No unresolved match, duplicate ranking row, unregistered entrant, anonymous champion, CPU age-eligibility violation, retired CPU entry or retired cardholder was flagged.

Reconstructing CPU season wins/losses/match counts from complete brackets found no arithmetic discrepancies. Recorded CPU major wins did not exceed their stored major counters, and active CPU match records did not have missing/zero highest breaks. These checks do not certify every simulated shot, every historical cash transaction or external real-world tournament regulations.

### Rankings and tour cards

The broader baseline verifier recalculated **1,539 earned card awards**: 453 federation-route awards, 271 Q Tour awards and 815 Q School awards. Every recipient had a next-season card. A narrower finals-only check covered 1,020 awards and also found none missing. These overlap and must not be added together.

Both final ranking lists exactly matched a rebuild from the saved earnings ledger. Two successive repairs left the tested cash, finance ledger, titles, world-player records, contracts, stories and match IDs unchanged. This checks consistency of surviving credits; it is not independent research into every event's real-world payout schedule.

### Generational turnover

All 2,326 newcomers were named. A generated newcomer first reached the top ten in 2043/44; Caleb Usman became the first generated newcomer to win the World Championship in 2052/53. Champions continued changing across later generations. The active world population stabilised around 1,500 while retired records remained archived.

### Form, staff, teams and media

Eight natural form episodes were recorded, and all resolved. Recorded episode durations were 30–45 days. One conclusion was recorded three days beyond the nominal six-week deadline; dated settlement can skip over that deadline. This is a timing follow-up, not proof that a penalty affected a match after expiry.

There were 872 staff story incidents, 136 team arcs, 72 recorded rival-return arcs, eight form arcs and one junior-coach arc. Four named CPU coach moves and four later returns survived in the history. This demonstrates the employment route works in some cases; early contract expiry prevented many other planned moves.

All 136 accepted side events completed, with 862 rubbers and 46 deciding doubles. The aggregate found no field-identity, event-cap, rubber-score, frame-count, unnecessary-doubles or winner-share flags. The retirement exception above remains a failure even though those events resolved successfully.

The 388 interviews and 1,089 storyline incidents were deduplicated across annual snapshots for counting. The manager generally praised opponents and declined renewals; alternate answers, negotiations, unmet promises and interrupted doubles reload are covered by separate feature tests, not comprehensively by this single run.

### Finances and save integrity

The balance monitor sampled 70,571 transitions. One youth debt episode reached **−£48.60** and recovered through the event result on the same game date. No completed season closed in debt. Final cash was approximately **£47.13 million**, so later decades offered little financial pressure for this very successful career.

Compression/decompression was lossless. Final-save checksum was unchanged after verification. Browser probes used clean isolated contexts and did not touch the live RT save. The baseline terminal compressed payload was **5,504,029 UTF-16 characters**; both active and named localStorage writes failed. [Storage result](/C:/dev/snooker_career/artifacts/century-20260911/storage-final-68.json).

## Checks that need interpretation

The old manager report warned about non-tour main-tour participation and world-ranking money after card loss. Inspection found **zero positive human world-ranking ledger awards after 2072/73**. For example, 2072/73 income included £17,500 from Champion of Champions with zero ranking credit. The generic report groups invitational participation/prize money too broadly; those warnings do not establish illegal ranking credit. Whether a decades-old title should remain an invitation route needs a separate invitation-policy review.

Finance history is capped and sampled at action boundaries. Neither this report nor a zero duplicate-ledger count proves every internal fee mutation was observed. Likewise, CPU rating variation and successful promotion in this one youth career do not establish balanced outcomes for all starting paths.
