# Every starting age through age 65 — career audit

Status: COMPLETE. 78/78 careers reached the requested cutoff.

## Main conclusion

The simulation can sustain full careers across the tested ages, but this is not a clean release sign-off. Three game defects were confirmed: protected tour-card holders can be excluded from qualifying below World #128; the human world-player season records count events as matches; and age 49 has no valid starting route, falling back to a junior configuration. Two audit-reporting defects also need correction: incomplete finance categories and duplicate heuristic warnings. Early youth difficulty, late-career competitiveness and accumulated wealth remain balance concerns.

No gameplay fixes were applied during this matrix. Every case used the same frozen engine; the findings below are reproducible follow-up work.

## Scope and reproducibility

Every individual starting age from 12 through 50, using seeds 104729 and 130363: 78 careers and 2,652 planned seasons. Each simulation stops when the human player first reaches age 65 at a season checkpoint. This is an audit cutoff; it does not force the game’s retirement flag or impose a retirement rule on CPU players. Actual retirement state is included in the career CSV.

All careers use the same frozen engine, standard difficulty, middle financial support, balanced automated management, rotating training and the actual entry-access rules. Starting paths vary with age: club junior 12–14; national youth 15–17; elite amateur 18–20; Q Tour 21–24; rookie professional 25–34; top-64 professional 35–45; bottom-tour professional 46–48; Masters/senior at 50. Age 49 has no valid route and falls back to the junior-club configuration—an identified defect. Requested and actual route IDs are both preserved in the CSV. Age comparisons therefore also reflect different starting paths. These runs do not test every possible age/path/difficulty combination.

Base commit: `a105d7ec11e3960297acfcab2f60e332ac78540e` plus the local fixes recorded by the manifest’s input-file hashes. Frozen engine SHA-256: `b3c723e6f3f31d4dca00a29e5eab958fb86d143ee0765ee7c42129d89092c7b8`.

Independent processes share no game state. CPU/world simulation, live-match evidence, season-life systems, finances and annual integrity checks are enabled. Raw reports, annual ledgers, final saves and random-generator states are retained under `artifacts/simulations`; process logs and source hashes are in the matrix manifest.

## Findings reviewed beyond the automated flags

### An active second-year Q School card does not preserve access to main-tour qualifiers when the two-year World Ranking falls below 128.

**confirmed; high priority.** A seven-season instrumented replay exactly matched all seven original season reports. On 2032-08-16 the player was #134 with hasTourCard=true, currentYear=2, yearsRemaining=1 and expiry 2032/33. English, International, UK, Welsh, World Open and World Championship qualifying were blocked across 37 recorded snapshots. Card loss was not recorded until 2033-06-30.

The player can lose a protected season of main-tour opportunities, weakening retention and promotion balance. This is distinct from legitimate card expiry.

### Age 49 has no valid starting career route

**Confirmed creation / fallback defect; high priority.** The catalogue allows Bottom Tour only through age 48 and Masters from age 50; every other route ends earlier. getEligibleStartingLevels returns an empty list at 49. getValidatedStartingLevel then returns the first catalogue entry, start-club-junior, despite its age limit of 16. NewCareerPage does not disable Start Career for this condition. The age-49 matrix cases exercise this fallback and must not be treated as valid senior starts. The completed fallback careers recorded only 1 win in 105 matches and 1 in 104.

Provide a valid age-49 route and stop invalid fallback configurations being silently accepted. The requested Masters route also legitimately resolves to Top 64 at 45 and Bottom Tour at 46–48; those actual routes are recorded in the CSV.

### The youngest starting path still produces long runs of defeats despite eventual career progression.

**observed balance concern; medium priority.** Seed 104729 recorded 14 wins in 142 matches from starting ages 12 through 17, including 0-23 at age 15. Seed 130363 recorded 25 wins in 137 matches in those years. Both ultimately earned professional cards and finished positive.

Financial survival and eventual promotion do not establish that the first several seasons are enjoyable. The automated manager may select events that are too difficult.

### Late starting careers stay solvent but provide very few wins or trophies under the automated manager.

**observed balance concern; medium priority.** The age-40 starts finished 49/207 and 47/226 for wins/matches; the age-50 starts finished 11/106 and 7/103. None of these four careers won a recorded singles title. All reached 65 with positive balances.

A career can remain technically playable while offering an unrewarding experience. Inspect accessible local/seniors competition strength and manager event selection before changing match fairness.

### Large savings still accumulate when the automated manager keeps ongoing costs very low.

**observed balance / audit-policy limitation; medium priority.** Over 48 seasons this career earned £5,968,259 prize money and £4,598,369 sponsor income, ending with £10,042,702. Recorded staff costs totalled £93,913 and facility costs were zero. Sponsorship fell from £106,894 in the age-49 season to £27,837 at 51 and £9,500 at 60; pre-existing signed contracts are honoured.

Cash remains positive and sponsorship falls after card loss, but elite-player spending may stop being meaningful. These are recorded audit categories and are not a complete reconciliation (see the separate finance-reporting defect). The audit manager chooses cheap staff and free facilities; review realistic management policies and optional costs before adding compulsory cash drains.

### The audit emits two warnings for the same World Championship win-rate statistic.

**confirmed audit-reporting defect; low priority.** Both warnings describe a 61.9% win rate in 42 main-draw matches with no World title. The career lost its World finals; this is possible and is not itself a game bug.

Count this as one heuristic concern, not two game defects. Raw warning rows remain available for transparency.

### Human world-player records count events as matches

**Confirmed record defect; high priority.** advanceWorldPlayersForNewSeason builds seasonRecord.matches from event counts while its wins/losses count matches; the evidence-based correction explicitly excludes the human. In age50 seed104729, the canonical first-season report correctly records 7 matches (3 wins, 4 losses), while the human world-player copy records 4 matches with the same 3 wins and 4 losses. Annual copies accumulate the mismatch. CPU rows pass this specific check.

The human profile headline uses careerLegacyOf and is protected, but playerSeasonHistory prefers the incorrect saved seasonal match count, so the human profile season table can be inconsistent. Repair the duplicate human world record and its old seasonal totals. This finding does not establish incorrect ranking credits; ranking awards use the result ledger.

### Audit finance categories do not fully reconcile to actual cash movement

**Confirmed audit-reporting defect; medium priority.** The age12 seed104729 first season changes cash by -£6,758.50, while audit categories sum to -£7,299.50: a £541 residual. Two instrumented replays exactly matched the original season report. £450 net team-event fees/awards plus £91 withdrawal refunds fully explain that residual. Both kinds of action change cash without updating the audit categories. Final cash comes directly from the save, not the category sum.

Treat the detailed category breakdown as incomplete. The reproduced discrepancy is in audit accounting, not money being created or lost in the game. Capture every action cash delta and separately classify team rewards, costs and refunds.

## Limits and files

All 78 runs stopped at age 65; none was flagged as retired by the game at that checkpoint. This verifies the requested test cutoff, not a new mandatory retirement mechanic. The two age-49 cases have invalid starting routes as documented above.

This is an automated simulation audit, not a browser usability or load-time benchmark. Long-save wall times include four concurrent processes and JSON audit export. The manager is consistent but does not represent every human strategy. Two seeds per age are broad coverage rather than a precise estimate of rare-event probabilities.

Companion CSVs provide every completed career, every season, individual attribute development, raw warnings and a separate reviewed-findings table. The season CSV includes unclassifiedCashChange so incomplete audit categories are visible. Final conclusions and release implications should be read alongside the issue review; heuristic balance warnings are not automatically confirmed defects.
