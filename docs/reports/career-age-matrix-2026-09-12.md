# Fresh-career age matrix — v0.1.2

Tested 12 September 2026. Source commit `a105d7ec11e3960297acfcab2f60e332ac78540e`.

**All 15 assigned careers completed: 470 core seasons, 48,880 event ledgers and 3,957,863 scored world/human bracket matches.** Fourteen careers ran for 30 seasons, starting at ages 12, 15, 18, 21, 30, 40 and 50 with two seeds each. A separate age-12 career ran for 50 seasons. Supplementary diagnosis added a three-season reproduction and one one-season comparison; those four seasons are excluded from the totals above.

**Not an all-clear.** The main confirmed gameplay defect is an entry-round/bracket mismatch in seeded Q Tour and senior events. Team individual point counters can also become negative after fouls. Young-start balance, late-career decline and financial pressure need review. Performance results below distinguish working storage from loading speed.

## Conditions and limits

- Fresh isolated saves; the user’s live career was not loaded, altered or advanced.
- Standard/default career difficulty, middle support, balanced automated management and rotating training. No synthetic skill/preparation boost or forced human wins.
- Human singles used the playable visit/frame engine with neutral session recovery; the wider CPU tour used its existing event-resolution engine. Human singles/doubles in pairs events were played through the visit engine, with teammate singles and other ties resolved by the team system.
- The manager accepts eligible pairs invitations, chooses training recovery, praises opponents in interviews, declines renewal demands and hires a junior coach if unstaffed. These runs do not exercise every dialogue choice or financial strategy.
- All cases start with £8,000 and the same underlying human background template. Age and starting pathway both vary: this is a coverage matrix, not an experiment isolating age alone.
- The manager limits annual entries and does not choose early voluntary retirement. The age-50 cases therefore exercise the automatic retirement fallback and two subsequent seasons.
- “Years” here means complete game seasons. The initial season starts in May 2026; later seasons follow the game’s calendar. Two seeds per age do not establish population-wide balance.
- CLI simulation retains full historical data. Its memory and elapsed time are not normal browser load measurements. Browser measurements use production builds and archived saves.

## Confirmed defects

### 1. Entry rounds can disagree with the seeded bracket

The audit found 3 human season records that disagree with the actual played bracket; 1 event had no completed final.

- **Age 40, Top-64 start, seed 130363, 2028/29, Europe – Event 7:** history records a 0–3 preliminary loss to Yusuf Kovacs, while the published draw places the player at seed 29 in the Last 128 against Gianni Quinnell. That actual match remains unscored. The final is Leo Shaw against TBD, yet the ledger is marked applied/completed on 14 February 2029.
- A fresh **three-season repetition reproduced this exact inconsistency**. It remained unresolved at season rollover. This is not merely an unfinished screen before the event’s publication date.
- **Age 40, seed 104729, 2029/30, same event:** the career records three matches and two wins, while the bracket contains two matches and one win. The extra preliminary win is absent from the draw.
- **Age 21 Q Tour start, seed 130363, 2046/47, World Seniors Championship:** a semi-final run is recorded as four matches / three wins, while the bracket contains three matches / two wins. The human is seeded fifth into the Last 16; another win appears only in the career record.
- The evidence points to entry-round selection and the prepared opponent disagreeing with a seeded bye. The first case can prevent downstream matches and awards from completing; the other two inflate the recorded match/win total. No retrospective payout repair was made in this testing task.

### 2. Team-event individual points subtract foul penalties

28 invalid individual-stat rows were found in the checked team records. For example, Bartosz El-Sayed finished a Youth Club Pairs singles match with **−5 points** after one foul even though his frame scores were both zero.

`src/game/seasonLife/live.ts` adds the signed visit-log points directly to the player’s points counter. A foul log is negative, so it reduces “Points” displayed in the team history. Separate the player’s scoring from penalties conceded; keep the opponent’s awarded foul points in the team/frame total. The audited tie winners and frame formats remained valid.

## Balance concerns, rather than proven rules defects

### Beginner careers are excessively punishing under this policy

The two age-12, 30-season careers won **9 of 275 matches (3.3%) before age 18**, against a weighted pre-match forecast around **29%**. One seed went through several winless seasons. Their first cards arrived at age 22, so eventual progression works, but the opening experience is poor.

The balanced manager spent heavily on equipment and also chose strong mixed-level events, so the loss rate is not an age-only comparison or proof that every young opponent is too strong.

A separate frugal one-season comparison finished with £3,721 and no observed debt, but won zero of nine matches. Its manager still selected some strong mixed-level events. This does not establish what a careful human restricting entry to suitable junior events would achieve.

Review age-appropriate event recommendations and opponent distribution, then calibrate the displayed forecast against the visit engine. Do not simply give the human a hidden win boost.

### Very late careers collapse to the attribute floor

The age-50 cases both finished at age 80 with overall 10. In one, by age 77 every attribute except Professionalism had fallen to 1; Professionalism remained above 93. Automatic retirement subsequently worked, and the next two seasons advanced without match participation.

The human can fall to an overall near 10 while CPU overall development has a floor of 35. This makes the final playable years particularly uncompetitive. Review the joint physical/technical decline model, floors and voluntary-retirement guidance; no NaNs or out-of-range attributes were found.

### Sponsorship and support can remove most financial pressure

Both age-50 starts won no singles titles, earned only £125 / £437.50 in lifetime tournament prizes, yet ended around £857k / £830k. Recorded sponsor income was roughly £662k / £626k. This is not a ranking-credit leak: it is a commercial/support balance question, especially alongside reputation reaching 100 despite poor singles results.

Retirement stopped new competitive participation. Previously signed sponsor contracts ran down over subsequent seasons; their continuing payments are not classified as a bug. The test manager also kept a coach, leaving a small recurring expense.

## Integrity checks that passed

- All 48,880 expected annual event ledgers survived, including Championship League across rollover. Ledger presence alone is not event completion: the incomplete Q Tour event above is explicitly excluded from an all-clear.
- Each year retained 128 professional card holders. **7,050** named Q School/play-off card awards were checked, with no missing next-season card.
- **355,593 CPU player-season records** were compared with actual bracket matches/wins/losses/draws. No CPU arithmetic discrepancies or missing observed major-title counters were found.
- No anonymous champions, duplicate player identities, duplicate ranking rows, retired entrants or invalid recorded break values were found. **661,760** age-limited entrant checks passed.
- All 15 final saves retained their human season summaries; season match totals and canonical trophy counts reconcile with career totals. This arithmetic pass does not erase the three bracket/history mismatches described above.
- **3,840 final world/one-year ranking rows** were independently recomputed from dated, unexpired earnings and matched the saved tables. This checks accounting against saved credits, not the correctness of an award missing because a draw was incomplete.
- 930 accepted pairs events completed, including 1,380 doubles rubbers. No excess seasonal invitations, duplicate participants, invalid tie formats, duplicate financial entries or contamination of singles records was flagged. Individual point counters have the separate defect above.
- 2,113 unique seasonal stories, 1,632 interviews and 2,920 staff-history entries were exercised. No early announced coach departure, sub-14-day new notice or simultaneous human/CPU coach employment was detected.
- An additional 697,480 group fixtures passed their configured score rules, including four-frame Championship League draws.
- Season-opening dates remained consistent with the saved opening snapshot. Human evidence/history windows stayed bounded; full historical season summaries and event records remained available.

## Audit-manager limitations and false positives

The automated World Championship selector still uses a historical “World Champion”/“Major Contender” status as a reason to protect the main draw. That can make it keep choosing an unavailable main draw instead of an available qualifier after the player drops outside the top 16. Two seasons in the age-12 seed-104729 career missed both routes.

A focused selector probe held date, rank (#24) and card constant: game access correctly rejected direct main entry and accepted qualifying; changing only the historical status switched the manager’s choice. This establishes a harness selection defect, not proof that the game’s eligibility gate admits an ineligible player. The probe is a controlled reconstruction, not an exact replay of those two season dates.

335 “resolve the inbox decision before playing” messages were expected action gates. The manager answered them and continued; they were not deadlocks. Heuristics warning about a World title at age 24, a top-16 opening rank later requiring qualifying, or a good win rate without enough titles are not by themselves game defects.
